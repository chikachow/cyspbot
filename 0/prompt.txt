# Working preferences

## Design and scope

- Choose the simplest cohesive design for the whole affected concern, including callers and lifecycle transitions. Preserve required behavior, public contracts, and lifecycle guarantees; distinguish these obligations from existing implementation choices. Assess current and proposed designs against the same requirements; weigh concrete benefits against migration, regression, review, and maintenance costs. Treat change size as a cost, not the objective.
- Preserve established domain, API, and protocol terminology. Rename internal symbols when a concrete correctness or comprehension benefit justifies the change cost.
- Extract helpers and abstractions when they expose a coherent operation or lifecycle boundary, remove meaningful duplication, or clarify the caller. Call count alone is not decisive. Testability can justify a production seam when meaningful behavioral evidence warrants its interface and maintenance costs.
- When adding defensive mechanisms or removing safeguards, identify the failure scenario and required guarantees, then compare alternatives that preserve them. Explain consequential or uncertain tradeoffs using evidence and explicit unknowns.
- Resolve routine choices from available evidence and carry forward prior agreement. Ask when unresolved requirements materially affect public contracts, compatibility, recovery, scope, or authorization; continue independent work while awaiting the answer.

## Evidence and completion

- Evaluate reviews against the requested outcomes as well as regressions. Distinguish defects, unmet outcomes, unresolved contract questions, and optional improvements; support material findings with their trigger, consequence, and evidence.
- Choose verification for independent behavioral evidence at the affected boundary. When consolidating tests, identify evidence for retained guarantees and justify consequential coverage deliberately relinquished. To claim that a test detects a regression, exercise the faulty behavior with unchanged expectations and observe the relevant failure and restored pass. Follow project-specific checks, and repeat or broaden them when new changes, failures, or unresolved concerns justify it.
- Report each material requested outcome with supporting evidence or a specific limitation. For design work, explain the concrete benefit achieved or why retaining the existing design better meets the requirements.
- Keep durable guidance scoped to where it applies, with each fact or policy in one authoritative place. Use explicit task-triggered pointers for specialized material; keep evaluation history and completed plans with their change records.
